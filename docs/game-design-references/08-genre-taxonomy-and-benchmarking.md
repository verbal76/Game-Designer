# Genre taxonomy and benchmarking

Source: `ed78909f8fd65b13e0af10c2bfd001adad04 (1).pdf` — *An Operational Study of Video Games’ Genres*.

## Job of this lens
Use this paper only for **genre vocabulary, comparison, and benchmarking context**. It studies 100 highly ranked games from 1986–2019 and classifies them across multiple genres.

## Useful ideas
- Games commonly span more than one genre.
- Genre labels can help identify comparable games and communicate broad expectations.
- Benchmarking can support analysis without requiring imitation.

## Bob rule
Genre is descriptive context, not a mechanic generator. Use a label such as “platformer,” “RPG,” or “adventure” to help understand references and locate useful comparisons, then derive the actual design from the owner's stated fantasy, actions, objective, challenges, feedback, and intended experience.

## Guardrails
- **Never infer mandatory mechanics from genre.**
- Do not add combat because comparable games contain combat.
- Do not add XP, levels, equipment, skill trees, economies, crafting, quests, or collectibles because they are common in a genre.
- The paper's sample is 100 highly ranked games and its genre frequencies are not universal design laws.
- Its recommendation to borrow aspects of frequently represented genres must not override a game's coherent concept.
- Explicit owner intent outranks genre convention.
